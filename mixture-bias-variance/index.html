3.5 · Mixture Models

Build a complex distribution by mixing simpler ones.

p(y) = Σk πk pk(y)
Mixing weight πkPrior chance of choosing component k. πk ≥ 0 and Σπk = 1.
choose z ~ π→choose component→generate y

z is latent: we observe y, but usually do not observe which component generated it.

Important: we do not generate one point from every component and average them. One component generates each point.

Responsibility

After seeing y, infer where it probably came from.

rnk = P(zn = k | yn, θ)
Prior vs posteriorπk = before seeing y.   rnk = after seeing this y.
ClusteringWhat you keep
SoftAll responsibilities, e.g. (0.2, 0.7, 0.1)
HardPick argmax → cluster 2

GMM: components are Gaussians. With enough components, a GMM can model complex smooth distributions and is commonly used for unsupervised clustering.

Special simplified GMM conditions connect hard assignment to the nearest centroid idea behind K-means.

4.7.6 · Bias–Variance Tradeoff

Imagine repeatedly drawing a new training dataset and refitting the model.

Too Simple · UnderfitHigh Bias
Low Variance
Useful BalanceControl total error
not either term alone
Too Flexible · OverfitLow Bias
High Variance
Bias = systematic errorHow far the estimator's average result is from the truth.
Bias(θ̂) = E[θ̂(D)] − θ*
Variance = instabilityHow much the estimate changes when the training dataset changes.

A model can be consistently wrong (high bias, low variance), or correct on average but unstable (low bias, high variance).

MSE = Variance + Bias²

The tradeoff: accepting some bias can be beneficial if it reduces variance by enough to lower the overall MSE. Stronger regularization typically raises bias and lowers variance; weaker regularization does the reverse.
